## Slow response

When this happens in real life: A customer taps Pay at a Lagos shop and the page keeps spinning. The payment service is working, but its answer is late. The customer taps again and wonders if the first payment went through. In another country, a shopper can leave when a page feels stuck.

What to check in your app: Show a clear waiting state and stop double taps. Set a time limit for the answer and decide what your app does when that limit passes. Check the payment status before you try again.

Settings:

| Setting | Type | Allowed | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `seconds` | Whole number | 1 to 120 | `5` |

Applies to:

- **Paystack**: Assumed: not confirmed by the service's documentation. Reference: research/paystack.md §14.2 says this needs a real story.
- **Flutterwave**: Docs. Reference: research/flutterwave.md §14.2 and §14.1.

## Forged webhook

When this happens in real life: A forged webhook reaches Ngozi's food shop in Enugu. It looks like a payment success, but the signature does not match. A signature is a code that proves a message came from the service. If the app trusts the message, an attacker can mark an unpaid order as paid. In an international shop, the same trick can unlock a download for free.

What to check in your app: Verify every webhook with the service's documented method before you trust it. Reject a missing or wrong signature and record the rejection. Never use the webhook body as proof of payment without that check.

| Setting | Type | Allowed | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `method` | Text with at least one character | Any valid value | `wrong-secret` |
